Crawl Space Water Removal: What Richardson Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Settling Under the House

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Rot You Cannot See

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.
