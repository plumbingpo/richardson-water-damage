Bathroom Water Damage in Richardson

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. Our Richardson team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • Peeling paint or swollen baseboard on the wall next to the shower
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Mold in the Tiled Wall

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.
