No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Richardson Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

Structural Drying: What Homeowners Ask

Your electricity use will go up while the commercial drying equipment is running. For the whole drying period, the extra cost typically comes to $50 to $150. It is usually reimbursable through your insurance claim as part of the restoration costs, depending on your policy.
Psychrometry is the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. IICRC-certified technicians on our staff apply it when deciding on temperature, airflow and the position of equipment. No two materials release moisture at the same rate, so the approach varies. Getting it right avoids both under-drying, which feeds mold, and over-drying, which can damage wood.
For most homes, structural drying runs 3 to 5 days. Building construction, ambient conditions, the materials involved and the degree of saturation all change how long it takes. Commercial projects or heavy saturation may take 5 to 7 days or more. Our technician checks progress every day and updates you on timing.
Placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage. Commercial LGR dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential units. High-velocity air movers also create the precise airflow needed to speed evaporation from structural surfaces. Household gear also comes with no way of measuring whether drying has finished.
Most of the time there is no need to leave while the structure dries. The equipment is safe but noisy. Ask us about ways to reduce the disruption, for example keeping units away from bedrooms or changing the airflow pattern. Whatever we arrange, the machines still need to run continuously.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
