Wet Carpet Drying in Richardson

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Richardson extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Stains From Furniture

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Damp Base of the Walls

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.

Subfloor Damage

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.
