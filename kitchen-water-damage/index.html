Kitchen Water Damage Help in Richardson

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. We help Richardson homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

What We Do

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Remove Only Damage

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.
