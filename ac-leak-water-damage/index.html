AC Leak Water Damage Help in Richardson

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • Drain pans that sit unevenly or have corroded and split.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Near the Return

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Floors Outside the Closet

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.
